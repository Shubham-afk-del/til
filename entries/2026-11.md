# November 2026

## 7 Nov 2026

- **Loss (mean squared error):** I computed MSE by hand with NumPy: `errors = actual - guess`, then `(errors**2).mean()`. For actual values `[50, 60, 70]`, the close guess `[48, 63, 69]` gives 4.67 and the far guess `[40, 75, 60]` gives 141.67. Squaring stops plus and minus errors from cancelling out and makes one big miss cost far more than several small ones, so a lower loss means predictions that sit closer to the real values.

## 6 Nov 2026

- **Encode and Decode Strings (decode by slicing):** I finished `decode`: `j = strs.index("#", i)` finds the next separator, `length = int(strs[i:j])` reads the number in front of it, `strs[j+1:j+1+length]` takes exactly that many characters, and `i = j + 1 + length` jumps to the next number. Reading by length is what makes it safe, because a `#` inside a word like `"a#b"` is just one of the counted characters, so my tests pass for that word, an empty string and an empty list.
- **LLM-as-judge (truncated JSON):** I built a small judge: I numbered the three models' answers with `enumerate(..., start=1)`, asked `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` on Groq to rank them and reply with JSON only, parsed the reply with `json.loads` and mapped each ranked number back to its model with `[n-1]`. On one run the judge's reason rambled and the reply stopped before its closing quote, probably at `max_tokens=500`, so `json.loads` failed with `JSONDecodeError`; the token budget has to fit the whole answer, and the reply needs a check before it is parsed.
